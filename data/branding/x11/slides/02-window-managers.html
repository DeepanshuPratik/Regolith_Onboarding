The window manager is the desktop

On X11, the choice you make on first login is the choice that defines the whole session. Three families cover most users; each does one thing well and stays out of the way of everything else.

  • FloatingOpenbox, Fluxbox. Windows are things you place, like papers on a desk. Familiar from Windows and macOS.
  • Tilingi3, dwm, awesome. Windows snap to a grid; you choose where, not how. The keyboard does most of the work.
  • StackedFVWM, WindowMaker. Windows overlap but raise when focused. A middle ground, less common today.
  • xinit ~/.xinitrcPick one explicitly. Whichever you choose, run it from your .xinitrc; that's the file startx reads.
  • update-alternatives --display x-window-managerSee what's installed. A quick way to tell what your distro made the default, if a DM hasn't shown you yet.

This is one of the freedoms that drove people to Linux in the first place. Lean into it.
