What X11 gives you, and what it doesn't

X11 is the protocol that lets windows talk to a keyboard and a mouse. It draws nothing of its own: no panel, no dock, no notifications. What you see on screen is whatever your window manager draws, plus whatever compositing effects a separate compositor adds on top.

  • Alt + TabCycle windows. Provided by every X11 window manager, in some form. The exact keys vary.
  • Ctrl + Alt + TOpen a terminal. A convention, not a guarantee. Most distros bind it by default; if yours doesn't, the launcher is one key away.
  • Alt + F2A command, without leaving the keyboard. Runs a single command through your shell; disappears.
  • Ctrl + Alt + ←/→Switch desktop. The convention from openbox and KDE 4 — most tiling WMs override it.
  • Print ScreenTake a screenshot. Handled by your screenshot tool; bind it if it isn't.

Transparency, animations and rounded corners all live in a separate compositor (picom, compton). If you want them, install one.
